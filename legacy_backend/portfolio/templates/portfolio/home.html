My Operating System

Obsession. Passion. Consistency.

Three words I try to practise, not just put on a wall.

01 / CURIOSITY

Obsession.

Understand it. Don't just make it work.

If something breaks, I want to know why — not just which command makes the error disappear. I like digging into problems until the pieces make sense. Sometimes this saves time. Sometimes I spend two hours investigating a five-minute problem. We're working on the efficiency part.

02 / ENERGY

Passion.

Build something. Learn something. Repeat.

I enjoy turning ideas into something real — whether that's a deployment pipeline, a Python application, a painting, or a meal. Passion makes me curious enough to begin and interested enough to keep improving.

03 / SHOWING UP

Consistency.

Progress doesn't require a perfect day.

Motivation has an unreliable attendance record. Consistency is what keeps me learning when the exciting part is over. Not every day has to be extraordinary. The important thing is to keep practising, correcting mistakes, and moving forward.

A rule I live by

I'm not afraid to start from zero.

I've already changed direction from Civil Engineering to technology. That meant being a beginner again, learning new tools, and accepting that I wouldn't know everything on day one. If there's something worth learning, I'm willing to start over. Ego wants to look experienced. Curiosity wants to get better. I know which one I'd rather feed.

The Origin Story

From construction sites to cloud infrastructure.

Different industries. Different tools. The same instinct to understand how things fit together.

CHAPTER 01 / THE FOUNDATION

Civil Engineering came first.

Learn to build things that stand.

I began with a Diploma in Civil Engineering and practical site experience. Working in Kerala and Saudi Arabia taught me about coordination, planning, deadlines, responsibility, and the real-world consequences of getting the details wrong.

The lesson: Good results depend on solid foundations and attention to detail.
CHAPTER 02 / THE TURNING POINT

Curiosity changed direction.

From physical structures to software systems.

My interest gradually shifted towards programming, automation, Linux, and cloud technology. I started learning Python and Django, then explored the infrastructure and delivery systems behind modern applications. It wasn't a shortcut; it was a decision to start learning a new field from the ground up.

The lesson: A career change doesn't erase your previous experience. It gives you a different foundation to build on.
CHAPTER 03 / THE BUILD

Learning by building.

Less watching. More doing.

I began working on practical projects involving Docker, Jenkins, Kubernetes, Terraform, AWS, Helm, Argo CD, security scanning, and observability. I've built CI/CD workflows, experimented with GitOps and progressive delivery, and learned to troubleshoot by working through real failures.

The lesson: Documentation is useful. Hands-on experience teaches you what the documentation couldn't predict.
CHAPTER 04 / RIGHT NOW

An engineer still in progress.

Building towards a career in DevOps.

Today, I'm focused on becoming a strong Junior DevOps Engineer. My goal is to deepen my understanding of cloud infrastructure, automation, secure delivery, reliability, and the systems that help development teams ship software with confidence.

The lesson: I'm not claiming to know everything. I'm committed to learning, building, and getting better.
The Question Everyone Asks

Will AI take over my job?

A reasonable concern. Also an excellent excuse to discuss my questionable career contingency plan.

AI

Honestly? AI is getting good.

I'm building my career in DevOps and modern cloud technology. I can see AI generating scripts, suggesting configurations, and helping troubleshoot errors. Ignoring that progress would be a questionable career strategy.

But generating a Terraform file isn't the same as deciding whether it belongs in production. Suggesting a Kubernetes fix isn't the same as understanding every consequence. Someone still needs to validate the output, understand the system, investigate failures, and take responsibility.

And if the machines take over anyway?

PLAN B

The Cook.

I can cook. I understand timing, patience, and the importance of not ignoring something on the stove. Very similar to production monitoring, except the alerts smell different.

PLAN C

The Artist.

I can paint, too. If the servers are run by robots, at least I'll have a creative outlet. Maybe I'll paint a portrait of the machine that replaced me. Freelance work is freelance work.

PLAN D

The Civil Engineer.

I have a Civil Engineering background and site experience. I understand the fundamentals of building design and construction. If the robots start building cities, someone should at least ask about the foundations.

My plan isn't to beat AI at being AI. It's to learn how to use it, question it, and build responsibly.

Jack of a few trades. Mastering my next one, one day at a time.
