Understanding How Structures Stand

Blueprints, material strengths, load paths, and site coordination.

I started in Civil Engineering, designing physical structures and coordinating site works. In civil projects, an overlooked variable or unverified calculation has real, tangible consequences. It taught me systems thinking, patience with complex blueprints, and the importance of solid foundations.

Core Takeaway: "No structure survives without a rigorous foundation — whether made of concrete or microservices."
PHASE 02 The Transition Blank Slate

Starting from Absolute Zero

Trading the hard hat for the terminal with zero fear of being a beginner.

Deciding to pivot from civil construction to technology was a conscious leap into the deep end. I was starting from scratch: learning Linux internals, bash scripting, networking protocols, and Python. Leaving behind past certainty to become a total beginner takes humility, but curiosity was louder.

Core Takeaway: "Ego wants to look experienced. Curiosity wants to get better. I know which one I'd rather feed."
PHASE 03 The DevOps Crucible Modern Era

Pipelines, Cloud & Scalable Systems

Discovering that infrastructure is civil engineering for electrons.

Suddenly, everything connected. Terraform configurations were digital blueprints; Docker containers were pre-cast modular units; CI/CD pipelines were automated assembly lines. I fell in love with automating deployments, managing distributed instances on AWS, container orchestration, and architecting resilient Python backends.

Core Takeaway: "If it has to be done more than twice, automate it before it consumes your sanity."
