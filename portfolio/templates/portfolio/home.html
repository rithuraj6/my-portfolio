My Operating Principles

Obsession. Passion. Consistency.

Three words I try to practise, not just put on a wall.

01 / CURIOSITY

Obsession.

I want to know how things work, why they break, and what happens if I try a different approach. Sometimes that solves the problem. Sometimes I spend far too long investigating it. Curiosity has questionable working hours.

02 / ENERGY

Passion.

I enjoy building, experimenting, and turning confusion into understanding. Whether it's infrastructure, code, a painting, or a new recipe, I like making something better than it was before.

03 / SHOWING UP

Consistency.

Motivation is great, but its attendance record is unreliable. Consistency keeps me learning and practising even when the exciting part is over. Every day doesn't have to be extraordinary; progress just has to continue.

A rule I live by

I'm not afraid to start from zero.

I've already changed direction from Civil Engineering to technology. If there's something worth learning, I'm willing to be a beginner again. Ego wants to look experienced. Curiosity wants to get better. I know which one I'd rather feed.

A Reasonable Concern

Will AI take my job?

Honestly, AI is getting scary good. Pretending otherwise would be a questionable career strategy.

I'm a DevOps Engineer working with modern cloud technologies, and I can already see AI writing scripts, generating configurations, and helping troubleshoot problems that used to take considerably longer.

But generating a Terraform configuration isn't the same as knowing whether it belongs in production. Suggesting a Kubernetes fix isn't the same as understanding its side effects. Building a pipeline is one thing; taking responsibility when a deployment goes sideways is another.

And if AI eventually decides DevOps is its territory now? I have a few backup plans.

I can cook. I can paint. I studied Civil Engineering, so I understand the fundamentals of designing and building structures. I'm a bit of a jack of all trades — not a master of all of them, let's keep the marketing honest.

If the machines take over the servers, I'll make dinner. If they take over the kitchen, I'll paint a portrait of our new robot overlords. And if all else fails, I'll ask them whether their buildings have passed structural review.

My plan isn't to compete with AI at being AI. It's to learn how to use it, question it, and take responsibility for what gets built with it.

Beyond the Terminal

A job title isn't a whole personality.

Because not every interesting thing in life requires a terminal window.

The Cook

I enjoy cooking. Debugging and cooking both require patience, timing, and accepting that sometimes you have to start over halfway through.

The Artist

The Civil Engineer

My Civil Engineering background and site experience taught me planning, coordination, precision, and the importance of getting the foundations right.
